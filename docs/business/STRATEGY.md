## 1. Honest commercial posture

```text
Orbit Yard failed as a commercial company thesis
(Integrations wedge · generic DevOps SKU).

What remains viable (see [PEER-BUSINESS-MODELS-2026](./PEER-BUSINESS-MODELS-2026.md)):
  a residual buyers cannot git-clone — paid/gated product, hosted control plane,
  or labor with a real channel — not “more OSS for visibility.”
  Personal sequencer utility still real; OSS-as-hook is falsified for this operator.

What is not viable:
  revive Integrations · compete on CI/host parity · AI finishes OAuth ·
  claim ops/deploy pivot = go-to-market.
```

This shelf exists to compound **sustainable value** under that verdict — not to deny it.

## 3. Business model alignment (dual trunk)

| Trunk | Offer | Harvest |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Gift / C-T** | Free OSS sequencing · MCP · CLI | Trust · dogfood · portfolio face |
| **Optional H1** | Concierge “first host URL live once” on *their* repo | Fixed fee later — only after proof pulse |
| **Paid license** | Account-holders who want the tool without DevOps fluency | Only if honesty + value bar still true; not Integrations |

Paid paths must not require Studio-held secrets, fake one-click past Login, or vendor-setup theater.
